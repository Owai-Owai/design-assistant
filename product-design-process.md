# Product Design Process Sub-Skill

This sub-skill supports the end-to-end product design process — from research
and ideation through prototyping, presenting, and iterating on designs.

---

## What This Sub-Skill Covers

- Writing design briefs and problem statements
- Structuring design research plans
- Synthesizing user research into insights
- Writing ideation and concept documentation
- Drafting design critique agendas and feedback requests
- Writing design presentation narratives
- Documenting design decisions and rationale

---

## Output Formats

### Design Brief
```
Project: [Name]
Date: [Date]

Problem Statement:
  [Clear one-paragraph description of the problem being solved]

Goals:
  - [Goal 1]
  - [Goal 2]

Success Metrics:
  - [How we'll know this worked]

Constraints:
  - [Technical, time, business constraints]

Out of Scope:
  - [What we are explicitly NOT solving]

Timeline:
  - [Key milestones]
```

### Research Plan
```
Research Goal: [What we want to learn]
Method: [Usability test / Interview / Survey / etc.]
Participants: [Who, how many, recruitment criteria]
Questions / Tasks:
  1. [Question or task]
  2. ...
Analysis Method: [How findings will be synthesized]
Output: [What deliverable comes out of this]
```

### Design Decision Log
```
Decision: [What was decided]
Date: [Date]
Context: [What prompted this decision]
Options Considered:
  - Option A: [Description + tradeoffs]
  - Option B: [Description + tradeoffs]
Chosen: [Option + reason]
Risks: [What could go wrong with this choice]
```

### Presentation Narrative
```
Opening: [Frame the problem — why does this matter?]
Context: [Research findings that informed the design]
Solution: [Walk through the design — what and why]
Tradeoffs: [What we considered and rejected]
Next Steps: [What feedback is needed, what comes next]
```

---

## Writing Guidelines

- Always frame design work around **user needs and business goals**
- Make design decisions explicit — never leave the "why" undocumented
- When presenting, lead with the problem before showing the solution
- Be honest about tradeoffs — strong designers acknowledge what they gave up
- Write for an audience that includes non-designers (PMs, engineers, stakeholders)

---

## Example Triggers

- "Write a design brief for this feature"
- "Help me structure a research plan"
- "Summarize these research findings into insights"
- "Write a narrative for my design presentation"
- "Document the design decisions we made"
